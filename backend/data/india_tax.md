## Capital gains on listed shares and equity mutual funds
keywords: ltcg stcg capital gains equity shares stocks equity mutual funds index funds elss etf sell selling holding period long-term short-term 112a 111a grandfathering stt

- Applies to listed equity shares, equity-oriented mutual funds (including index funds and ELSS) and units of business trusts, where securities transaction tax (STT) is paid.
- Holding period: long-term if held for more than 12 months; otherwise short-term.
- Long-term capital gains (LTCG): taxed at 12.5% on total gains above ₹1.25 lakh in a financial year, for sales on or after 23 July 2024 (earlier: 10% above ₹1 lakh). No indexation.
- Short-term capital gains (STCG): taxed at 20% for sales on or after 23 July 2024 (earlier: 15%).
- Shares bought before 1 February 2018 are grandfathered: the cost is the higher of the actual cost and the market price on 31 January 2018 (but not more than the sale price).
- Health and education cess of 4% applies on top of the tax.
- The new-regime rebate that makes income up to ₹12 lakh tax-free does not cover these special-rate capital gains.

## Capital losses and tax-loss harvesting
keywords: loss losses set off carry forward harvesting harvest book profit tax harvesting ltcl stcl

- A short-term capital loss can be set off against both short-term and long-term gains.
- A long-term capital loss can be set off only against long-term gains.
- Unused capital losses can be carried forward for 8 years, but only if the income-tax return is filed by the due date.
- Tax harvesting: investors often sell equity holdings to book up to ₹1.25 lakh of long-term gains each year (which are tax-free) and buy back, raising their cost. India currently has no wash-sale rule, but brokerage and STT costs apply.

## Debt mutual funds, bonds and other assets
keywords: debt fund debt mutual fund liquid fund bonds debentures gold property real estate land unlisted indexation 50aa 24 months slab

- Debt mutual funds bought on or after 1 April 2023: gains are added to income and taxed at the investor's slab rate, whatever the holding period. There is no long-term benefit or indexation.
- Listed bonds and debentures: long-term if held more than 12 months; LTCG taxed at 12.5% without indexation.
- Unlisted shares, physical gold and real estate: long-term if held more than 24 months; LTCG taxed at 12.5% without indexation for sales on or after 23 July 2024.
- Land or buildings bought before 23 July 2024: resident individuals and HUFs can choose the lower of 12.5% without indexation or 20% with indexation.

## Dividends and share buybacks
keywords: dividend dividends dividend income tds buyback buy-back interest

- Dividends are added to income and taxed at the investor's slab rate.
- Companies and mutual funds deduct 10% TDS when dividends paid to a resident individual exceed ₹10,000 in a financial year (the threshold was ₹5,000 until 31 March 2025).
- Interest paid on money borrowed to earn dividends can be deducted, up to 20% of the dividend income.
- From 1 October 2024, money received in a company's share buyback is taxed as dividend in the shareholder's hands; the cost of the shares bought back becomes a capital loss.

## Income-tax regimes and slab rates
keywords: slab slabs tax rate income tax new regime old regime rebate 87a standard deduction salaried cess surcharge which regime

- The new tax regime is the default. FY 2025-26 slabs: up to ₹4 lakh nil; ₹4–8 lakh 5%; ₹8–12 lakh 10%; ₹12–16 lakh 15%; ₹16–20 lakh 20%; ₹20–24 lakh 25%; above ₹24 lakh 30%.
- New regime: a rebate makes tax nil for taxable income up to ₹12 lakh (₹12.75 lakh for salaried people after the ₹75,000 standard deduction). The rebate does not apply to special-rate income such as STCG on equity.
- Old regime (below 60 years): up to ₹2.5 lakh nil; ₹2.5–5 lakh 5%; ₹5–10 lakh 20%; above ₹10 lakh 30%; standard deduction ₹50,000; tax nil up to ₹5 lakh taxable income. It allows deductions such as 80C, 80D, HRA and home-loan interest.
- A 4% health and education cess applies in both regimes; surcharge applies at high incomes.
- Which is better depends on how many deductions you can claim; compare both every year.

## Tax-saving investments (old regime)
keywords: 80c 80d 80ccd nps elss ppf epf vpf nsc tax saving tax-saving fd insurance premium sukanya deduction lock-in save tax

- Section 80C: up to ₹1.5 lakh a year combined across ELSS funds, PPF, EPF/VPF contributions, NSC, 5-year tax-saving bank FDs, life insurance premiums, home-loan principal, children's tuition fees and Sukanya Samriddhi.
- ELSS has a 3-year lock-in, the shortest among 80C options; gains are taxed like other equity funds when sold.
- NPS: an extra deduction of up to ₹50,000 under 80CCD(1B), over and above 80C.
- Section 80D: health insurance premiums up to ₹25,000 (₹50,000 for senior citizens), with a separate limit for parents' cover.
- Most of these deductions are not available in the new regime. Employer contributions to NPS (80CCD(2)) are allowed in the new regime, up to 14% of salary.

## PPF, EPF, NPS and Sovereign Gold Bonds
keywords: ppf epf provident fund nps pension annuity sgb sovereign gold bond gold bonds retirement interest

- PPF: contributions of ₹500 to ₹1.5 lakh a year, 15-year term, and interest and maturity are tax-free (EEE). The government resets the interest rate every quarter — check the current rate.
- EPF: interest is tax-free, except interest on employee contributions above ₹2.5 lakh a year (₹5 lakh if the employer doesn't contribute), which is taxable.
- NPS: at 60, up to 60% of the corpus can be withdrawn tax-free; at least 40% must buy an annuity, whose pension is taxed at slab rates.
- Sovereign Gold Bonds: the 2.5% annual interest is taxed at slab rates. Capital gains are tax-free when an individual redeems at maturity (8 years); bonds sold on the stock exchange are taxed as listed securities.

## Trading, advance tax and filing
keywords: intraday f&o futures options trading business income advance tax itr filing due date speculative stt

- Intraday equity trading is speculative business income; futures and options trading is non-speculative business income. Both are taxed at slab rates, not capital-gains rates.
- Advance tax is due if the year's tax after TDS exceeds ₹10,000, including tax on capital gains. Instalments are due by 15 June, 15 September, 15 December and 15 March.
- The usual return-filing deadline for individuals without an audit is 31 July; filing on time is needed to carry forward losses.
